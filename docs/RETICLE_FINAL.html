01 — why sequencing isn't enough

Sequencing gives you the parts list — not what the parts do.

what the sequence tells you
  • every gene, located and named
  • ~20,000 protein-coding genes
  • how one person's letters differ from another's
what it doesn't
  • what each gene actually does
  • what happens to a cell when it's lost
  • which genes matter, and under which conditions
A parts list won't tell you which part the engine can't run without — for that, you have to remove one and see what breaks.
01 — what a CRISPR screen is

A CRISPR screen switches off every gene — one per cell.

CRISPR mechanism diagram / animation still
assets/crispr_mechanism.png
Cas9 guided to one gene per cell — a library of guides covers the whole genome
the experiment, in three steps
  • perturbA guide library disables each gene — one per cell, across millions of cells
  • selectThe pool grows for weeks — on its own, or under a drug or stress
  • read outSequence before and after — which knockouts vanished, which took over
dropped out → the gene was essential
took over → losing it helped

This is rare data: the measured result of actually breaking each gene — cause, not correlation. Every screen ends the same way: one score per gene.

02 — and the data itself doesn't line up

Thousands of screens exist — but no two speak the same language.

Context is missing

01 — can't compare

What a score means — what was perturbed, under what stress, in which cells — is usually left in the methods section. So rank #132 in one screen is not rank #132 in another.

Cutoffs hide the buffered genes

02 — can't see

Every screen draws a line: above it are "hits," the rest is discarded. Genes whose function is backed up by a partner are real but modest — and always fall below the line.

Absence is read as zero

03 — can't trust

Many screens deposit only their hit list. A missing gene might have scored near zero, or might never have been measured — opposite facts that no current method tells apart.

Together: an enormous evidence base that is effectively write-only — deposited, and never reused in aggregate.
